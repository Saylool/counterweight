# Price source and fallback — v3 revision, 2026-11-10

Deployed keyless v2 retains the immutable-band exit bug. The following
changes apply to the separate deployed keyless [v3 policy](POLICY-V3.md).

Pyth's [official NEAR integration page](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/core/use-real-time-data/pull-integration/near) says NEAR support ended on August 26, 2026. Its [current contract registry](https://docs.pyth.network/pro/contract-addresses) no longer supplies supported NEAR testnet or mainnet IDs. Consequently neither network has a currently maintained ID to verify there. The old `pyth-oracle.testnet` / `pyth-oracle.near` names are historical, not recommended live endpoints; the proposed init explicitly uses `account_id: null`. No testnet transaction was sent to investigate availability.

The NEAR/USD **Core/Hermes** feed ID is `c415de8d2eba7db216527dff4b60e8f3a5311c740dadb233e13e12547e226750` (note `2eba`, not `2efa`). Verified against the official [symbols endpoint](https://pyth.dourolabs.app/v1/symbols?query=NEAR&asset_type=crypto), `Crypto.NEAR/USD`, exponent -8, Pro/Lazer ID 27. The [feed-ID documentation](https://docs.pyth.network/pro/price-feed-ids) identifies `hermes_id` as the Core identifier; Pro numeric IDs are not interchangeable.

`refresh_oracle` is public. If an explicitly approved Pyth-compatible contract is configured, it calls only that immutable account's `get_price_no_older_than(price_id, age)` and its private callback parses the official NEAR wire shape: decimal-string price/confidence, numeric exponent and publish timestamp. It rechecks publication age/future time, positive price, exponent/overflow, confidence ≤ configured basis points, the wide sanity range (10–1,000,000,000,000 micro-USD) and monotone oracle publication times. Null, failure, oversized, malformed, stale or high-uncertainty responses do not refresh the cache. Fresh oracle prices take precedence for opens, reductions, sales and margin health; caller reduction hints are ignored. Reads never attest venue fills/equity. No production oracle deployment is claimed by mock tests.

While oracle is absent or stale, `set_keeper_price(price, observed_at)` accepts only the designated keeper, a non-future observation at most `max_age_seconds` old, and an observation no older than the fallback cache. Keeper and owner price caches accept the wide sanity range so exits and margin health remain usable outside the opening band. Opens enforce the current `[min_price,max_price]` at signing, even when a cache or fresh oracle lies outside it. A cache update cannot change the band. Owner may propose a new range, cancel it, or execute it after 24 hours; replacing a proposal restarts the delay. These contract methods remain callable after the policy's access keys are deleted.

Reductions never depend on that band or a pending proposal. A fresh public oracle takes precedence; otherwise caller hints require `observed_at` seconds at most `min(60,max_age_seconds)` old. Without a hint a fresh cached fallback may be used. Exit prices must lie in the wide $0.00001–$1,000,000 sanity range and be representable on the venue tick. Buy reduce-only IOC prices are always at most mark+1%, **rounded down**, independently of the opening slippage setting. Caller hints never refresh opening permissions. Read [POLICY-V3](POLICY-V3.md) for exact units, formula, ABI, limits and tests.

This is **not an independently verified market oracle**. With no authenticated feed, keeper/public prices and timestamps are attestations; a false high mark can authorize an adverse buyback. The 1% bound holds against the reported mark, not an independently observed market. Timestamp checks do not prove observation truth. Venue liquidity, signer availability, fees and gas can still prevent an exit. The old dummy $0.95–$1.05 proof configuration must not be funded. The funded v2 deployment has a different $2.784560–$4.176840 band and cannot receive these fixes.

Tests cover the callback through real sandbox receipts, absent/stale/high-confidence oracle results, private callback rejection, out-of-order publications, fallback freshness, non-ratcheting endpoints and oracle precedence over hostile reduction hints.
